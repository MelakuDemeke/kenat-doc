# Kenat Moves to kenat.et, With a New Look

<img src="/blog/kenat-et-cover.png" alt="A new home, a new look: kenat-doc.vercel.app becomes kenat.et" className="w-full rounded-xl border border-zinc-200 dark:border-zinc-800 my-6" />

The Kenat site has a new address: **[kenat.et](https://kenat.et/)**.

An Ethiopian calendar library now lives on an Ethiopian domain. It is short enough to say out loud, easy to type on a phone, and it finally sounds like the project instead of a hosting provider. Everything that lived at `kenat-doc.vercel.app` is here now, and old links forward to the new address, so nothing you bookmarked or shared breaks.

The move came with a redesign, a set of new pages, and a place to show off the apps that run on Kenat. Here is what changed.

---

## A New Look

The old site leaned on glass panels and gradients. It looked like a lot of other sites. The new one is deliberately quiet: white and near-black surfaces, the original sky blue as the only accent, Geist for text and Noto Sans Ethiopic for Amharic.

The Ethiopian identity comes from real content rather than decoration. The first thing you see on the home page is today's date, computed live by the library in your browser: the Amharic weekday and month, the Ge'ez numerals, the Gregorian twin, the Ethiopian time and the next holiday.

A few other things came along with it:

- **A real Ethiopian clock.** The time converter and the playground now use an SVG clock face with an Ethiopian hour dial, in Ge'ez or Arabic numerals.
- **Everyday time words.** Times are labelled ጠዋት, ከሰዓት, ምሽት and ሌሊት, the way people actually say them, instead of a flat day and night.
- **Calmer tools, blog and docs.** The tools and the blog are simple card grids, and every blog post is dated in both calendars.
- **Light and dark.** Both themes were designed together, not one derived from the other.

## Ethiopian Dates, Answered Directly

Most people who need an Ethiopian date are not developers. They want to know what today is, when Fasika falls this year, or how long ago Enkutatash was. So the site now answers those questions directly, on pages built for them.

### Today in Ethiopia

**[kenat.et/today](/today)** shows today's date in Addis Ababa: the Ethiopian date in English, Amharic and Ge'ez numerals, the current Ethiopian time, how many days are left in the year, and the next holidays. It updates live, so it is right even if you leave the tab open overnight.

<figure className="my-6">
  <img src="/blog/kenat-et/today.png" alt="The Today in Ethiopia page" className="w-full rounded-xl border border-zinc-200 dark:border-zinc-800" />
</figure>

### Every Holiday, With a Countdown

**[kenat.et/holidays](/holidays)** lists every holiday this year and next in both calendars. Each holiday also has its own page: [Enkutatash](/holidays/enkutatash), [Meskel](/holidays/meskel), [Genna](/holidays/genna), [Timket](/holidays/timket), [Adwa](/holidays/adwa), [Fasika](/holidays/fasika), [Eid al-Fitr](/holidays/eid-al-fitr), [Eid al-Adha](/holidays/eid-al-adha) and fifteen more.

Every holiday page tells you how many days are left until the next one, how long ago the last one was, and its dates for past and future years. Movable feasts come from Bahire Hasab, and the Muslim holidays come from the Hijri calendar, exactly as the library computes them.

<figure className="my-6">
  <img src="/blog/kenat-et/holiday.png" alt="The Enkutatash page with a countdown to the next Ethiopian New Year" className="w-full rounded-xl border border-zinc-200 dark:border-zinc-800" />
</figure>

### The Whole Year, Month by Month

**[kenat.et/calendar](/calendar)** shows all 13 months of the current year, Meskerem through Pagume, with the Gregorian date under every day and the holidays marked. Every year from 2010 to 2030 has its own page and holiday list, and every single date links to a page with that day in both calendars.

<figure className="my-6">
  <img src="/blog/kenat-et/calendar.png" alt="The 2019 Ethiopian calendar page, month by month" className="w-full rounded-xl border border-zinc-200 dark:border-zinc-800" />
</figure>

Every one of these pages is generated by `kenat` itself. If a date is wrong there, it is wrong in the library, and it gets fixed for everyone at once.

There is a small detail for the developers behind these apps. Every Play Store link on kenat.et carries an install referrer, so installs from the showcase show up with `kenat.et` as their source in Play Console. If Kenat sends you users, you can see it.

### Add Yours

Built something with Kenat? An app, a website, a bot, a widget, anything. [Open a showcase issue on GitHub](https://github.com/MelakuDemeke/kenat/issues/new?title=Showcase%3A%20) <FiExternalLink className="inline-block" /> with a link and a line about what it does, and it will join the list on the home page.

## What Stays the Same

The docs, the tools and the libraries are exactly where you left them, just at a new address:

- [Documentation](/doc) for JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Dart, PHP and the CLI
- [Tools](/tools): date converter, time converter, month grid, Bahire Hasab and the holiday browser
- [Blog](/blog) and release notes

If you link to Kenat from your own project's README or site, please update the link to **https://kenat.et**. The old one still works, but the new one is shorter and it is the one that will stay.
